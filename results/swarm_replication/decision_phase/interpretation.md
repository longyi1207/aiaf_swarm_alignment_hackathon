# What changed when evidence analysis was spared?

All 24 games finished using byte-identical copies of the eight main worlds. L6 and
matched random L6 were active during prediction, discussion and filing only. Forty
auditors per arm produced the following outcomes.

| Arm | Found own fabrication | External | Internal | Missing | Audits / 8 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| None | 38 | 24 | 16 | 0 | 5 |
| L6 after analysis | 40 | 7 | 3 | 30 | 1 |
| Random L6 after analysis | 38 | 16 | 24 | 0 | 3 |

Compared with whole-game L6, correct discovery rose from 5/40 to 40/40. External
submissions moved from six to seven and missing submissions rose from 24 to 30.
The external-filing deficit relative to matched random was 13/40 in the full-game
intervention and 9/40 in this study. Each estimate uses only two world seeds. The
repeated unlesioned baselines both gave 24 external filings, while 14 matched
individuals switched channels. These comparisons describe live trajectories.

Offline inspection locates a distinction the final counts obscure. Among auditors with
missing submissions, one in the full-game L6 arm had produced a valid external call
after a narrow end-tag correction, versus 14 after intact analysis. The model often
reached a usable evidence citation but failed to encode the filing channel correctly.
Reported audits use only filings the original interface actually accepted.
